Why it matters

Capability grows. Authority must be designed.

Artificial intelligence is rapidly moving beyond answering questions and generating content. AI agents can now analyze, decide, communicate, access enterprise systems, delegate work, initiate transactions, and take action.

That creates a new challenge for every organization: how do you give AI enough authority to create value, without surrendering human control? The more capable the agent becomes, the more important its authority boundaries become. Expotech uses AIM™ to establish those boundaries before autonomy is expanded.

An agent may be capable of
  • Accessing databases
  • Communicating with customers
  • Generating purchase orders
  • Modifying business records
  • Interacting with other agents
  • Initiating actions across multiple enterprise systems
The authority envelope

Eight dimensions of agent authority

AIM™ answers not only what an AI agent can do, but the more important question: what should it be allowed to do?

Purpose

The permitted purpose the agent serves.

Data

What information it may access.

Action

Which actions it may take.

Financial

Whether it can spend or transact, and how much.

Time

When it is allowed to operate.

Delegation

Whether it can hand authority to another agent.

System

Which systems it may reach.

Consequence

The boundaries on what its actions can cause.

The AIM™ lifecycle

From concept to retirement

AIM™ supports AI throughout its lifecycle, from concept and development through deployment, operation, monitoring, and eventual retirement.

The objective is not to prevent AI from acting. It is to make certain that AI acts within the authority humans intended to give it.

  1. 01Discover

    Find where AI agents are already operating, or being proposed, across the organization.

  2. 02Classify

    Classify agents according to capability, autonomy, access, and potential consequences.

  3. 03Design

    Design appropriate authority boundaries and human controls.

  4. 04Authorize

    Authorize specific actions, systems, data, financial limits, and delegation rights.

  5. 05Monitor

    Monitor agent behavior and detect movement outside approved boundaries.

  6. 06Intervene

    Intervene when predetermined red lines or tripwires are reached.

  7. 07Revoke

    Revoke authority and return systems to a safe state when necessary.

The book behind the methodology

The Last Command

Building Powerful AI Without Surrendering Human Control

By Dr. Raed (Rod) Ghani Agha. The book examines the transition from AI as a tool to AI as an autonomous actor within organizations. It introduces AIM™ and explores the architecture, governance principles, authority boundaries, risk models, organizational controls, and leadership practices required to deploy powerful AI without unintentionally transferring control from people to machines.

Written for executives, technology leaders, AI developers, cybersecurity professionals, business leaders, educators, and students.
